What each verifier looks at
  • geometry parts are valid solids, inside the envelope, mass and vendor CAD agree
  • interference no two parts overlap; screws reach thread and do not bottom out
  • tolerance 3,000 simulated builds: do screws start, do gaps close, is there enough adjustment
  • manufacture each made part can be cut, printed or machined as drawn
  • ratings motors, guides, belts and bearings are inside their makers' ratings
  • motion nothing collides anywhere in the travel, belts route, drives have margin
  • subsystems homing switches, bed probe, cables, the things a printer needs
  • electrical wiring, power budget, mains safety, heater faults, the generated firmware config
  • assemblability an order exists in which every part can be placed and every screw driven
  • fea stiffness and stress of loaded parts and assemblies, heated bed, bed support
  • printing a test print on the dynamic model of the machine
  • requirements the design's own stated requirements

Probe coverage

The probe measures the bed's height so the firmware can follow its shape. It can only measure where it can reach, and it is not where the nozzle is.

probedinside the print area, never probednozzle at the cornerprobe at that moment

What was checked. The subsystems verifier works out where the probe can stand over the plate from its offset, the axis travel and the margin its sensing field needs from the plate edge. Both drawings are the same scale, front of the machine at the bottom. What it means. v7 left strips at the right and front that were never measured; the firmware holds the nearest measured value there. v8 puts the probe straight behind the nozzle and gives Y the extra travel, so the whole print area is measured. On a bed assumed tilted 0.3 and 0.2 mm and bowed 0.15 mm, the first-layer gap error over the print area is 0.097 mm RMS (0.305 worst) without the mesh and 0.007 mm RMS (0.017 worst) with it, using Klipper's own mesh interpolation. Assumed. That a thin spring-steel sheet on a magnet switches the sensor at the 5 mm its data sheet gives for an iron target. That is the first thing to test on a bench.

What is still unverified

Passing every check does not make the machine real. These are the things no check covers, taken word for word from docs/PRINTER_V8.md.

  • That thin spring steel on a magnet switches the sensor like OMRON's iron target. If it switches at 3 mm instead of 5, the window closes to nothing at a 2 mm face height: check on the bench before anything else.
  • Lead differences between the three screws, and so how evenly they carry.
  • The flexure posts: fatigue of a threaded M5 bent 0.3 mm at each heat cycle; that the jam nuts stay tight.
  • The bed's own vibration on four slender posts and a tie. No modal case was written for it.
  • Pulley set screws carrying each screw's axial load (10 to 20 N) on a plain 8 mm screw end.
  • The printed pedestals and mount under years of load (PETG creep); bearing seats printed 22.4 mm.
  • 2 mm steel Y mounts: stiffness under 37 N of belt pull is not in any FEA case.
  • The frame: 50 mm taller, front top rail lowered. Frame stiffness does not enter the twin.
  • The Z belt's 1,056 mm stock length; a toothed idler running on the belt's back.
  • Air: the duct is v7's, 10 mm further right, with the fan on its upper holes.
  • The probe mount's stiffness (not in an FEA case) and heat on it 11 mm behind the Roto.
  • Everything v7 listed that v8 did not touch, and everything AGENT.md lists as not checked.

Beyond this list: the frame's own stiffness does not enter the dynamic model, the tolerance spreads are typical values and not a supplier's, and no number on this page has been compared with a measurement of this machine, because there is no machine.
