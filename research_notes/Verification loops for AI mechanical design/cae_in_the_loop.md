## 1. Closed-loop reference systems: what they do, what feedback they give, what it buys

### Takeaway
The published 2026 loops converge on the same shape — deterministic fixed FEA kit (almost always Gmsh/netgen -> CalculiX, C3D10) run by a controller, compact structured text/JSON feedback to the LLM, typed pass/fail requirement checkers — and all report that first-attempt artifacts almost never satisfy strict physics requirements, with FEA feedback giving real but modest gains (partial-credit up by roughly 4-10 points per round; target-safety-factor hit rate ~59% with FEA feedback in one study). No published system does bolted/sliding-contact assembly FEA in the loop: assemblies are handled as bonded (conformal or tied) multi-material bodies.

### Cited Findings

**Hephaestus-CCX — "Self-Improving CAD Generation Agents with Finite Element Analysis as Feedback" (arXiv 2605.17448, Son, Park, Park, Ahn, Yu; May 2026)** — all [search]
- Task: engineering brief -> assembled (multi-part) STEP artifact. Benchmark is 50 prompt-and-requirement pairs (20 single-part, 30 multi-part) sampled from a 466-case pool; each case ships a fixed CalculiX evaluation kit and typed pass/fail requirement checkers. — [arXiv 2605.17448 v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17448v1), [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/405378679_Self-Improving_CAD_Generation_Agents_with_Finite_Element_Analysis_as_Feedback)
- Requirement types: briefs are decomposed into an explicitly typed requirement list (one example brief expands into six requirements); a figure caption says structural-analysis criteria dominate, with buckling, vibration, thermal, dimensional, geometric and material-compliance checks each a meaningful share. — [arXiv 2605.17448 v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17448v1)
- Metrics: "Strict pass" counts a case only when every typed requirement passes; "Mean requirement pass" averages the per-case fraction of requirements passed over the 50 cases. — [arXiv 2605.17448 v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17448v1)
- Loop: the agent (Codex / Claude Code / OpenCode harnesses) writes CadQuery, exports STEP; the controller runs the fixed CalculiX kit on the STEP and writes "a compact CCX feedback report" for repair; the paper additionally studies a structured "blueprint" step and a rich-view (reported as 21-view) visual inspection tool/image judge; up to 10 revision attempts are described in one summary. — [arXiv 2605.17448 v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17448v2), [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17448)
- Results: the paper states Hephaestus-CCX is "nearly unsolved under strict FEA grading". OpenCode baseline (no FEA retry) for GPT-5.5 (high): 0/20 single-part and 1/30 multi-part strict passes; Codex GPT-5.5: 0/30 multi-part before retry. A secondary Substack summary says zero strict passes across 400 first-attempt sweeps (conflicts slightly with the 1/30 in the paper's own table). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17448); contradicted in detail by [Substack summary](https://bretkerr.substack.com/p/the-silicon-foundry-how-2026-finally)
- Feedback benefit: one reported rise is 19.4% -> 29.3% mean requirement pass (attributed in a secondary source to GPT-5.5 with rich-view feedback; snippet from the paper does not name the setup). Rich-view image judge: ~+6.3 points mean requirement pass for GPT; blueprinting ~+3.9 points for Claude Code but nearly flat for GPT. Repeating the feedback loop compounds gains; nine artifacts become strict passes in the longest GPT-5.5/high feedback run. — [arXiv 2605.17448 v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17448v1), [Substack summary](https://bretkerr.substack.com/p/the-silicon-foundry-how-2026-finally)
- Cost/time: average model time scales from about 10 minutes to nearly 70 minutes per item as feedback loops lengthen. — [arXiv 2605.17448 v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17448v1)
- Failure-mode detail: some strict-pass flips were "checker-contract repairs" — displacement and buckling already passed by large margins but strict grading failed because the checker could not bind the mass requirement (i.e., artifact did not expose what the checker needed, e.g. material/part mapping). — [arXiv 2605.17448 v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17448v1)
- Independent use of H-CCX prompts (Reify 0.1.1 audit, Oct 2026, GitHub issue) [GH-read]: three H-CCX cases produced valid STEP assemblies (3, 36 and 47 solids) but only "partial" outcomes — e.g. 36 valid solids with 38 intersecting pairs and unholed fastener interfaces; a 47-solid sandwich panel with a homogeneous core lacking effective material mapping; no FEA was run. Author's conclusion: "Validity alone is not a sufficient completion criterion"; one case showed a false pass from success flags despite a dowel-hole contract failure. — [QiuYi111/Reify issue #37](https://github.com/QiuYi111/Reify/issues/37)

**Physics-in-the-Loop (arXiv 2605.19717; IJCAI-26 pp. 6417-6425; Berger, Usama, Mehlstäubl, Saske, Paetzold-Byhain, TU Dresden)** — all [search]
- "Generate-Simulate-Refine" loop with VLM agents (Planner, CAD Engineer, Structural Reviewer); no training; deterministic "validated knowledge-based" FEA tool that agents may inspect but not modify. — [IJCAI proceedings](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/714), [arXiv 2605.19717 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19717v1)
- Feedback: the Structural Reviewer runs FEA, computes minimum safety factor (yield / max stress), checks it against a target band [2, 5]; if outside, it gives "specific structural feedback, identifying stress hotspots or areas of over-engineering" to Planner and CAD Engineer. So the band penalises both under- and over-design. — [arXiv 2605.19717 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19717v1)
- Measured benefit: with FEA feedback the target SF band was reached in 49/83 successes (59.0%); the FEA-disabled arm appears as "6/27" in a truncated snippet (≈22%, unverified context). Abstract claims 4.2x structural complexity and +3.5% compile rate vs similar agentic methods. — [arXiv PDF 2605.19717](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19717)
- Failure modes: more iterations make results less stable; detecting an unrecoverable design is flagged as future work; Claude Sonnet 4.5 often produced geometries that were hard to mesh, lowering FEA success. — [IJCAI proceedings](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/714), [arXiv PDF 2605.19717](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19717)

**CADEngBench physics track, CADEngBench-P (arXiv 2608.09296, Singh, Dubey, Amador Herrera; 10 Aug 2026)** — all [search]
- 300 parametric parts, each with one zero-to-CAD and one functional-editing task; eight multimodal code-capable models evaluated; companion CADEngBench-A covers assembly grounding and kinematic verification over 150 body pairs. — [arXiv 2608.09296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09296)
- L3 = matched linear-static FEA: the generated part and a reference part are solved under the same loads/BCs in CalculiX 2.21 with Gmsh 4.15.2 and C3D10 tets. Compared quantities: 95th-percentile von Mises (not the max — a built-in singularity filter), max displacement, and normalised compliance or stress concentration depending on load case; disagreement = log ratio generated/reference, worst case across the parameter family. Absolute gates: stress utilisation <= 1.0, normalised displacement <= 0.02. — [arXiv 2608.09296 v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09296v1)
- A parameter family passes only if every state yields valid BCs, a valid mesh, a CalculiX solution, meets the limits, and stays within all comparison tolerances. "L3 reach" (FEA could run) is reported separately from "pair pass" (also agrees with reference). Best-model L3 matched-FEA pair pass reported as 46.3% (via pith.science summary; per-model table was garbled in the search extract). — [arXiv 2608.09296 v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09296v1), [pith.science summary](https://pith.science/paper/2608.09296)
- Qualitative: editing existing CAD is much easier than zero-to-CAD; generated parameter families can diverge from the reference structural response. Note an earlier, different ICML 2026 "CADEngBench" (40 tasks) used closed-form engineering surrogates at L3 rather than FEA — do not mix the numbers. — [arXiv 2608.09296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09296), [ICML 2026 listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/73498)

**MDO agent (arXiv 2511.17511, Guo et al.; arXiv listing dated Oct 2025; targeted at Advanced Engineering Informatics)** — all [search]
- Designer / Modeler / Verifier / Optimizer agents with human-in-the-loop; RAG for concepts; Modeler writes CadQuery; Verifier runs FEA through ReAct tool calls; Optimizer uses GA / Bayesian optimisation and handles contour-plot post-processing. Three cases: gas-turbine blade, machine-tool column (target < 1.70 t mass and < 25 µm deformation at 3 kN), fractal heat sink. — [arXiv 2511.17511](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.17511)
- Self-reported automation: FEA automation 82% overall (another snippet says ~70% of the FEA workflow) and "not fully successful"; optimisation-script generation 100%. I.e. FEA setup is the weakest automated stage. — [arXiv 2511.17511 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17511v1)

**FeaGPT (arXiv 2510.21993, Qi, Xu, Chu; Oct 2025)** — all [search]
- Geometry-Mesh-Simulation-Analysis (GMSA) pipeline from natural language; LLM emits structured JSON specs that are translated deterministically into CalculiX decks; stack FreeCAD 0.21, Gmsh 4.11.1, CalculiX 2.20. — [arXiv 2510.21993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21993), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21993v1)
- Validation: turbocharger 7-blade compressor and 12-blade turbine at 110,000 rpm; parametric NACA4412 study of 432 configurations (3x6x6x2x2) with 432/432 CalculiX convergence, 11 Pareto-optimal (2.5%). Wall-clock reported inconsistently (≈4 h in one version, ≈7.2 h in another). Results restricted to linear elasticity; no independent reference-solution comparison found. It is an FEA-automation pipeline, not a CAD-revision loop. — [arXiv 2510.21993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21993), [third-party critique in arXiv 2606.10928](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10928)

**earthtojake/text-to-cad FEA skill** — [GH-read], the most concrete open reference implementation
- Repo: MIT, 26.2k stars, 1,510 commits; agent skills for build123d CAD, manufacturing checks, FEA, drawings, URDF/MJCF. FEA skill = linear static stress study on STEP via `cad step solve`, netgen quadratic tets (C3D10) + bundled CalculiX 2.23 (>= 2.20 accepted via `CAD_CCX`), one thread, direct solver, private folder, "so results are reproducible". Explicitly "a first-pass engineering check, not a certification"; out of scope: vibration, buckling, heat, flow, fatigue, impact, sliding contact, bolts, bolt preload, remote/bearing loads, moments. — [repo README](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad), [skills/fea/SKILL.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/SKILL.md), [references/study-file.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/study-file.md)
- Loop for the agent: (1) list faces (`cad step solve part.step` with no study prints parts, touching pairs, and per-face ref/area/centre/surface type/hint); (2) write `study.json` (fixtures `fixed`/`roller`; loads `force` total-N, `pressure` MPa, `gravity`/`acceleration` in g; optional `margin` default 2, `checks`, `mesh`); (3) solve; (4) report. — [SKILL.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/SKILL.md)
- Feedback to the LLM is structured JSON plus images: `result.json` with `ok`, `findings[]` (`severity`, `code`, `message`, `location`, `hint`), `measurements` (nodal and integration-point max von Mises, safety factor, max displacement + location, applied vs reaction forces, per-fixture reactions, mesh/DOF/timings, `refined`), `checks[]` (each with `value`, `limit`, `ratio`, `status` fails/close/passes, `where`); plus `stress.png`/`displacement.png`, `result.vtu`, `result.glb`, optional `ccx.inp`. Exit code 1 on any error finding. — [study-file.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/study-file.md)
- Guards (pre-solve refusal): unknown keys, malformed study, load on fixed face, part with no fixture path, face wholly covered by a bonded joint, study that no load reaches (peak stress zero), rollers that do not fully restrain; post-solve refusal when reactions do not balance applied load. — [study-file.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/study-file.md), [SKILL.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/SKILL.md)

### Inferences
- Design pattern shared by every credible system: the LLM never writes solver decks freehand in the loop; it writes CAD + a small declarative study spec (JSON), and a deterministic kit does meshing/solve/extraction. FeaGPT, Hephaestus, Physics-in-the-Loop, CADEngBench and text-to-cad all sit on this side; the MDO agent, where the LLM generates simulation code, reports the lowest FEA automation (70-82%).
- Feedback format that demonstrably works is compact, typed and numeric (requirement id, value, limit, pass/fail, location/hint). Images help as an additional channel for geometry inspection (Hephaestus rich-view judge: ~+6 points) rather than as the carrier of stress numbers.
- A two-sided target (SF in [2,5], or mass limit plus stiffness limit) is needed, otherwise the loop trivially "fixes" stress by adding material; Physics-in-the-Loop's band and Hephaestus's mass requirement both do this.
- Expect per-item wall time to be dominated by LLM turns, not solves (10 -> 70 min per item in Hephaestus as loops lengthen), and expect instability with many iterations; a stop rule / "unrecoverable design" detector and keep-best-so-far logic are worth building.
- The "checker cannot bind the requirement" failure (Hephaestus) and "success flags despite contract failure" (Reify audit) imply the artifact must carry machine-readable part names, materials and named faces, and the verifier must check requested geometry directly rather than trust the agent's self-report.

## 2. Assembly FEA automation in open tools (5-30 parts)

### Takeaway
What is robustly automatable today is the "bonded assembly": per-part materials, automatic touching-pair detection from CAD proximity, one conformal multi-material mesh (or *TIE), connectivity-to-ground checks, per-part safety factors — exactly what text-to-cad ships. Frictional contact with bolt preload is scriptable in CalculiX (pygccx has classes and bolt helpers) but is fragile (convergence, tet-mesh pretension pitfalls) and no open agent loop runs it unattended; treat it as a tier-3, human-reviewed analysis and cover fasteners with analytic checks fed by FEA interface forces instead.

### Cited Findings
- text-to-cad assembly handling [GH-read]: touching parts are bonded into one conforming mesh; each part keeps its own material/density and is judged against its own yield; `weakest_part` reported. Detection tolerance `contact_tolerance_mm` default 0.1 (gaps within it are closed and reported `gap_closed`; gaps up to 1 mm are reported as not connected; overlaps no thicker than tolerance are bonded as interference fits; thicker overlaps are never bonded and are reported in `overlaps`). `connections` can force a pair `free`; a `free` pair still joined through other bonded parts is refused. Every part must reach a fixed part through bonded neighbours, else `not_connected` error (this is the rigid-body-mode guard, done topologically before solving). Result lists each connection's parts, type, area, gap, `interference_mm`, interface faces. Stated caveats: "Bonded joints are stiffer than bolted ones and exaggerate edge stress"; unbonded parts carry no load between them. — [study-file.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/study-file.md), [linear-static.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/linear-static.md)
- CalculiX contact formulations [search, from the CCX manual]: with quadratic elements, face-to-face penalty or mortar contact is recommended over node-to-face — "The face-to-face penalty method and the mortar method converge much better than the node-to-face method"; mortar in CalculiX is static-only. Debug convergence via the `.sta` (iterations per increment) and `.cvg` (contact element count, residual force, largest change) files and "last iterations" output. — [CCX 2.21 manual](http://www.dhondt.de/ccx_2.21.pdf)
- *TIE and contact are never automatic in CalculiX itself: "in each case you have to add it manually"; *TIE master must be a face-based surface; cgx has a `neigh` command with tolerance that auto-generates contact surface files (pre-processor-side detection). Practitioner view: a conformal mesh is often quicker than TIE; for nonlinear runs TIE is acceptable for small interfaces with a tolerance. — [CalculiX discourse](https://calculix.discourse.group/t/mesh-partitioning-for-hex-tet-combination-in-ccx/534), [R. Smith tutorial](https://rsmith.home.xs4all.nl/engineering/fea-with-calculix-2.html), [Mecway forum](https://mecway.com/forum/discussion/comment/3956) [search]
- Gmsh conformal multi-body: the Gmsh maintainer's advice is BooleanFragments so touching volumes share interface nodes (demo `compsolid.geo`); physical groups can then be applied to the fragment outputs. Keeping bodies distinguishable after fragmenting requires tracking the fragment output map (thread did not resolve it). — [Gmsh mailing list](https://onelab.info/pipermail/gmsh/2018/011942.html) [search]
- Bolt preload in CalculiX [search + GH-read]:
  - `*PRE-TENSION SECTION` exists; its MPCs are linear and do not follow large rotations; to let bolt load vary under external load, fix the pretension node's DOF at its current value in the following step. — [CalculiX discourse: bolted connections](https://calculix.discourse.group/t/bolted-connections/2548), [pretension thread](https://calculix.discourse.group/t/pretension-shear-tensile-resistance/2091)
  - Tet-mesh pitfall: the pretension section only separates elements that have a face on the cut; tets touching the cut only by edge/corner keep the load path so "the preload is largely lost" — use hex/wedge at the cut or verify preload with a section print. — [PrePoLix PR #208](https://github.com/KwentiN-ui/PrePoLix/pull/208)
  - Official example `NonLinear/Bolt` (half model, two plates, two M10 bolts, hole 11 mm): nonlinear static, surface-to-surface penalty contact, preload by thermal shrinkage (step 1 cool the bolt, step 2 displacement-controlled pull); pipeline is `gmsh Bolt.geo` -> `cgx -b pre.fbd` -> `ccx thermal`; tested with ccx/cgx 2.19; results are images only. [GH-read] — [CalculiX-Examples NonLinear/Bolt](https://github.com/calculix/CalculiX-Examples/tree/master/NonLinear/Bolt)
  - Alternatives: `*INITIAL CONDITIONS, TYPE=STRESS` needs trial runs with section print to hit a target force; force-applied preload behaves badly after joint separation. — [CalculiX discourse: pre-stretch](https://calculix.discourse.group/t/pre-stretch-in-calculix/715) [search]
- pygccx [GH-read]: Python framework (GPL-3.0, 203 commits, Python >= 3.10, lists CalculiX 2.22) wrapping Gmsh + CalculiX for 3D solids only ("no shells or beams"); keyword classes include CONTACT PAIR, SURFACE INTERACTION, SURFACE BEHAVIOR, FRICTION, CLEARANCE, GAP, TIE, MPC, EQUATION, COUPLING, DISTRIBUTING COUPLING, RIGID BODY, PRE-TENSION SECTION; steps STATIC/BUCKLE/FREQUENCY/VISCO; helper tools for "generating preloaded bolts from solid elements", section forces/moments post-processing, stress invariants, coordinate transforms, `.frd`/`.dat` readers. Examples folder includes `single_bolt`, `bolted_flange` (with `model.step`, `model.geo`, post script), `crowned_roller`, `crankshaft`, `beam_modal`, `beam_buckling`. — [calculix/pygccx](https://github.com/calculix/pygccx), [examples](https://github.com/calculix/pygccx/tree/master/examples)
- Rigid-body-mode detection [search]: forum practice is a free-free/"grounding check" frequency analysis — look for (near-)0 Hz modes to find unconstrained or disconnected parts; PrePoMax users describe cleaning contacts until only the expected six zero modes appear in a free-free run. — [CalculiX discourse](https://calculix.discourse.group/t/contacts-diverge-with-rigid-bodies-and-truss/2130), [PrePoMax discourse](https://prepomax.discourse.group/t/difference-in-results/1153?page=2)
- Frictionless contact leaves rigid-body modes unsuppressed; a static solve may fail or converge to machine-dependent answers through a near-zero pivot; standard remedies are weak springs or contact/nonlinear stabilisation (Ansys documentation, general FE principle). — [Ansys help](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/views/secured/corp/v251/en/ans_tec/teccoilspringintro.html) [search]
- Known CalculiX weak spots: rigid-body constraints with shells + NLGEOM converge poorly; shell-shell contact regressions reported in newer releases. — [CalculiX discourse](https://calculix.discourse.group/t/nlgeom-convergence-issue/2165/10), [discourse: shell pipes contact](https://calculix.discourse.group/t/contact-of-two-shell-pipes-no-convergence-in-newer-ccx-releases/2865) [search]
- Equivalents: FEniCSx contact is via asimov-contact (`dolfinx_contact`) — described by users as made for two-body problems on a single mesh object, mainly linear-elastic, with version friction against dolfinx 0.10; Code_Aster offers `DEFI_CONTACT` for contact and `AFFE_CHAR_MECA/LIAISON_MAIL` for tied non-matching meshes (a user fixed "pivot presque nul" singular errors by adding LIAISON_MAIL); no scripted bolt-preload example found. — [FEniCS discourse](https://fenicsproject.discourse.group/t/contact-between-two-deformable-bodies/19645), [Code_Aster docs u2.04.04](https://docaster-en-codeaster-doc-4113bb35b8213385c636b94f1536856d7f617.gitlab.io/manuals/man_u/u2/u2.04.04/M_thodologies.html), [CAELinux forum](https://www.caelinux.com/CMS3/index.php/forum/5-finite-element-modelling-codeaster-/6913-contacts-between-parts-in-thermal-analysis) [search]
- FreeCAD-driven parametric FEA wrapper exists: FreeCAD4ParametricFEA (CalculiX + Gmsh, controllable mesh, point/segment extraction with stress linearisation, Excel DoE). — [GitHub](https://github.com/Philippe-AMUZUGA/FreeCAD4ParametricFEA) [search]

### Inferences
- Realistic automation ladder for a 5-30 part assembly in an agent loop:
  1. Always: geometric pre-checks (interference, touching-pair graph, connectivity to ground) — cheap and catches the dominant real failures (the Reify audit found 38 intersecting pairs and unholed fastener interfaces in one generated assembly).
  2. Default FEA: bonded conformal multi-material linear static + modal (BooleanFragments or netgen-style conformal meshing, per-part element sets/materials). Robust, direct-solver, no convergence risk. Interpret as an upper bound on stiffness and a non-conservative picture near joints.
  3. Joint-aware linear: replace bonding at bolted interfaces by tying only washer-footprint annuli (or bolt "spiders": *COUPLING / *DISTRIBUTING COUPLING to a beam), extract interface/section forces per bolt, and run VDI-2230-style analytic bolt checks on those forces. Still linear, still automatable. (This combination is my synthesis; pygccx provides the coupling, section-force and bolt helper pieces, but I found no published agent doing it.)
  4. Full frictional contact + pretension: only for a few critical joints, with hex/wedge bolt shanks or thermal-shrink preload verified by section print, face-to-face penalty contact, weak-spring stabilisation, and human review.
- A free-free modal run on the bonded assembly is a cheap universal "is everything connected" test: more than six ~0 Hz modes means a floating part; fewer means over-constraint was meshed in.
- Because bonded joints overstate stiffness, a loop that optimises against bonded-assembly deflection or first-mode targets will be optimistic; a knock-down or joint-stiffness sensitivity (bonded vs washer-annulus-tied) run gives a bracket.

### Gaps
- No quantitative evidence found on convergence success rates of automated contact setup in CalculiX for multi-part assemblies.
- pygccx bolt-example source was not readable in detail (directory listing only); exact bolt-helper API not confirmed.
- No maintained "cqfem"-style build123d/CadQuery-native assembly FEA wrapper was identified besides text-to-cad's `cad step solve` and the CadQuery assembly-mesh-plugin; PrePoMax scripting on headless Linux was not investigated (PrePoMax is a Windows GUI). A project named "PrePoLix" (KOSMO-Engineering) appeared in search with PRs about matching CalculiX penalty/mortar contact and tet pretension, but its page returned 404 and its status is unknown.
- Code_Aster scripted bolt preload (e.g. PRE_SIGM / PRE_EPSI workflows) not documented from a reliable source here.

## 3. Carrying boundary conditions from CAD to mesh when geometry changes every iteration

### Takeaway
Face integer IDs are not stable across regenerations, so the durable options are (a) named/tagged faces authored in the CAD script that propagate to mesh groups, or (b) re-listing faces each revision with geometric descriptors and selecting by query — and then verifying the selection numerically (areas, applied vs reaction force) before trusting the solve.

### Cited Findings
- text-to-cad [GH-read]: face refs are `part/face N`, or named faces `part/f:plate.relief.3`; docs warn "Face numbers can change when a file is re-saved, so re-list faces before re-running on a new revision". The listing step prints per-face area, centre, surface type and a hint (e.g. base = largest planar face with downward normal; holes appear as cylinders); "Mirror-image faces match equally well, so ask rather than guess". `result.json.measurements.named_faces` echoes the resolved face references. — [study-file.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/study-file.md), [linear-static.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/linear-static.md)
- Load-case spec format in text-to-cad [GH-read]: one JSON object; total force spread uniformly over combined face area; pressure positive into the part; body loads in g with per-part density; `checks` typed as `stress` / `displacement` (with `limit_mm`, optional `faces`, `label`); strict schema — unknown keys refused with the valid keys named, all checks before meshing. Applied-force bookkeeping includes body loads so reactions must balance `applied_force_N`. — [study-file.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/study-file.md)
- CadQuery assembly-mesh-plugin [GH-read]: converts a `cq.Assembly` with tagged faces to a Gmsh mesh; tags become physical groups named `<partname>_<tag>`; a `~` prefix drops the part name so faces from several parts share one group (e.g. `~in_contact`) — intended for multi-material interfaces; `assy.saveToGmsh(mesh_path=...)` or `assy.getTaggedGmsh()` to keep the live Gmsh model for custom sizing. Tagged faces are matched to Gmsh surfaces by position. README limitations: tags live on Workplane objects not on Faces; OCCT has no native low-level tagging; PyPI install crashes when OCC objects are passed to Gmsh in memory, so conda/mamba is recommended; small project (16 stars, 83 commits, no releases listed); CadQuery only, no build123d mention. — [CadQuery/assembly-mesh-plugin](https://github.com/CadQuery/assembly-mesh-plugin)
- Classic STEP->Gmsh route is fragile: physical groups are "defined based on the surface/volume number assigned when the STEP file is merged", so numbers shift with geometry; if any physical group is defined Gmsh writes only elements belonging to groups; Gmsh also writes 2D surface elements for physical surfaces that must be stripped before CalculiX. — [R. Smith: FEA based on STEP geometry using Gmsh and CalculiX](https://rsmith.home.xs4all.nl/engineering/fea-based-on-step-geometry-using-gmsh-and-calculix.html), [CalculiX discourse](https://calculix.discourse.group/t/gmsh-and-only-c3d10-elements/2859) [search]
- Query-based selection: bounding-box selection in Gmsh should be padded because entities exactly on the box boundary can fail from floating-point error; yapCAD implements predicate-based face naming (normal-based selectors etc.) stored in solid metadata and propagated to Gmsh physical groups. — [apeGmsh selection guide](https://nmorabowen.github.io/apeGmsh/architecture/gmsh_selection/), [yapCAD face_naming](https://yapcad.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/yapcad/package/analysis/face_naming.html) [search]
- CADEngBench-P treats "valid boundary conditions" as an explicit gate distinct from "valid mesh" and "solution" for every parameter state of a family. — [arXiv 2608.09296 v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09296v1) [search]

### Inferences
- Recommended contract for a build123d loop: the CAD script itself emits a sidecar (JSON) of named interfaces, each defined by a selector expression evaluated at build time (e.g. "faces of part X, planar, normal -Z, min Z") together with expected area and centroid; the mesher maps these to physical groups by geometric matching (centre-of-mass + area + surface type within tolerance) after fragmenting, not by index. The verifier then asserts: number of faces matched, summed mesh area vs CAD area (e.g. <1%), load resultant and moment about a reference vs intended, and reaction sum = -applied.
- Selection ambiguity (mirror-symmetric candidates) should be surfaced to the agent as an error rather than resolved silently — the same rule text-to-cad gives agents.
- Keep the load case in a geometry-independent file (named interfaces + magnitudes + checks) so the same study re-binds to each revision; fail the iteration if a named interface disappears.

### Gaps
- No documented, tested recipe was found for in-process build123d (OCP) -> Gmsh transfer with tag preservation (e.g. via `importShapesNativePointer`); the only evidence is the CadQuery plugin's note that in-memory transfer crashes with PyPI wheels. Needs local testing.
- No standardised open load-case schema exists across tools; text-to-cad's `study.json` is the most complete open example found.

## 4. Speed and tiering for loops

### Takeaway
Linear-static C3D10 solves in the 100-250k DOF range take seconds to about a minute on one thread, so the solver is not the bottleneck in an LLM loop; the practical tiering is analytic sanity check -> coarse default mesh -> one automatic refinement only when margin is low, with hard DOF caps, and optimisation delegated to a numeric optimiser over CAD parameters rather than to LLM turns.

### Cited Findings
- Reference timings from text-to-cad [GH-read] (CalculiX 2.23, C3D10, direct solver, one thread): about 10 s and 1.8 GB at 140,000 DOF; 74 s and 4.3 GB at 207,000 DOF; warning above 150,000 DOF; solves over one hour are stopped. Default element size = 1/40 of bounding-box diagonal (max size), with automatic local refinement at thin walls/fillets/small holes. "Halving [size] roughly multiplies run time by 8." — [linear-static.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/linear-static.md), [study-file.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/study-file.md)
- Adaptive tiering rule in text-to-cad [GH-read]: only if safety factor < 3 does it re-mesh at half element size and re-solve; the finer run is capped at 150,000 DOF where possible and never exceeds 250,000; if peak changes > 10% it flags `mesh_not_converged`; both solves are recorded in `refined`. — [linear-static.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/linear-static.md)
- Hand-calc pre/post check [GH-read]: "Check the answer by hand first" — cantilever bending, ~3x nominal stress at a hole in tension, p·r/t for a thin ring; within about 20% of the estimate means the model is likely right; a larger error points to load/fixture/unit mistakes, to be checked in the order reactions -> face selection -> material. Body-load reaction check: density x volume x 9806.65 x g-vector (N). — [linear-static.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/linear-static.md)
- Solver choice for large models [search, forum reports, conflicting]: PaStiX has the best multi-core utilisation but reports say it becomes slower than PARDISO above ~1.0-1.1 million nodes; one bolt-assembly test: PaStiX 36.9 s vs PARDISO ~60 s; another user found PARDISO slightly faster in most tests; SPOOLES is memory-limited for large models and may use one core; PaStiX tuning: OMP_NUM_THREADS=6 beat 8 by 20%, OpenBLAS threads = 1, mixed precision on. — [CalculiX discourse: PaStiX performance](https://calculix.discourse.group/t/pastix-solver-performance/2525), [PaStiX vs PARDISO](https://calculix.discourse.group/t/pastix-vs-pardiso-performance/177), [Mecway forum](https://mecway.com/forum/discussion/comment/4522)
- Parameter sweeps: FeaGPT ran a 432-configuration Cartesian sweep with 100% CalculiX convergence in roughly 4-7 h wall-clock (inconsistent between versions) and extracted 11 Pareto-optimal designs. — [arXiv 2510.21993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21993) [search]
- Optimiser delegation: the MDO agent's Optimizer generates an executable Python optimisation loop (GA, Bayesian optimisation) around the CAD+FEA evaluation; script generation was the most reliable stage (100%). — [arXiv 2511.17511 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17511v1) [search]
- Topology optimisation agents (2026): TO-Master (deterministic FE/TO tools + LLM orchestration with intermediate mesh/BC checks) and TopOptAgents (FEniCS + pyOptSparse/SNOPT); neither search result describes hand-back to parametric CAD. — [arXiv 2607.01812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01812), [arXiv 2605.23273](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23273) [search]
- Linear scaling shortcut [GH-read]: because stress and displacement scale linearly with load, a check `ratio` of 0.5 means the load could double — one solve answers "what load fails" without re-running. — [study-file.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/study-file.md)

### Inferences
- Tier plan for the loop (synthesis): T0 geometry/mass/interference and section-property hand calcs (ms); T1 coarse C3D10 linear static, <=150k DOF (seconds-1 min); T1b modal on the same mesh (few modes, Lanczos; same order of cost as a handful of static solves); T2 single refinement when SF < ~3 or a `peak_concentration` flag is raised; T3 nonlinear/contact/buckling only on demand, since each increment costs a full factorisation and convergence is uncertain.
- With one-thread determinism (text-to-cad's choice) results are bit-reproducible, which matters for caching and regression tests; hash (geometry, study, mesh settings) -> result to skip re-solves when the agent revises something unrelated.
- For multi-load-case studies, reuse one factorisation: put load cases as multiple *STEPs on the same mesh/BCs; with linear scaling and superposition, unit-load cases can be combined analytically.
- Use the LLM for topology/feature decisions and an optimiser (Optuna/CMA-ES/BoTorch) for continuous parameters once the script is parametric; published loops that let the LLM hand-tune dimensions over many turns pay 10-70 min per item.

### Gaps
- No controlled CalculiX timing benchmark for 500k-1M DOF on a few cores was found; only forum anecdotes (and node counts vs DOF are often conflated). Needs local measurement.
- No sources retrieved on incremental remeshing, reduced-order/surrogate shortcuts in a CAD loop, FEniCSx-adjoint gradient use with CAD parameters, or open tools for converting topology-optimisation output back to parametric CAD — these questions remain unanswered by this research pass.
- Relative cost of modal vs buckling vs nonlinear in CalculiX is stated here only as inference, not from a cited benchmark.

## 5. Other physics verifiers for machines (3D printer / CNC gantry)

### Takeaway
Modal/stiffness of frames is directly reachable with the same CalculiX kit (FREQUENCY step), but open, maintained Python implementations of the classic machine-element standards are thin: there is an LGPL gear-rating library, and only tiny or brand-new repos for VDI 2230 and ISO 281 — a team should expect to implement and unit-test these calculators itself.

### Cited Findings
- Modal FEA limits for printers: a 2021 IEEE study modal-analysed an Anycubic i3 Mega in Fusion 360 and found the model "was incapable of predicting some low-frequency high-amplitude vibrations"; a 2022 Applied Sciences paper (open access) estimates printer natural frequencies analytically from the kinematic scheme and validates on the same printer; other frame/gantry studies use ANSYS modal + harmonic analysis. No CalculiX/FreeCAD-specific printer study was found. — [Applied Sciences 12(9):4514](https://mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/9/4514), [AMiner: Mechanical Vibration Analysis of a Gantry 3D Printer](https://aminer.org/pub/60b9a4dee4510cd7c8fc5ee3/mechanical-vibration-analysis-of-a-gantry-d-printer) [search]
- text-to-cad's FEA skill explicitly excludes vibration, buckling, heat, flow, fatigue and impact — i.e. the leading open agent skill stops at linear static. — [SKILL.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/SKILL.md) [GH-read]
- pygccx supports STATIC, BUCKLE, FREQUENCY and VISCO steps and CREEP/PLASTIC/HYPERELASTIC material keywords from Python, with `beam_modal`, `beam_buckling`, `beam_creep` examples. — [calculix/pygccx](https://github.com/calculix/pygccx) [GH-read]
- Hephaestus-CCX requirement types include buckling, vibration and thermal checks solved by the CalculiX kit — evidence these are automatable as typed checkers. — [arXiv 2605.17448 v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17448v1) [search]
- VDI 2230 in open Python: GitHub search returns only two tiny repos — `sdrummond-eng/fastener-strength-tool` ("SAE J429, ISO 898-1, VDI 2230", Python, 0 stars) and `Come2World/BoltPreMaster` (script to assist FEM bolted-joint analysis per VDI 2230, 0 stars, Aug 2025). Established implementations are commercial (HEXAGON SR1, KISSsoft, MDESIGN, SDC Verifier, CADFEM Bolt Assessment). — [GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=VDI+2230+python&type=repositories) [GH-read], [SDC Verifier VDI 2230](https://sdcverifier.com/engineering-standards/vdi-standards/vdi-2230-part-1-2015/) [search]
- ISO 281 bearing life in open Python: GitHub search returns one repo, `JRaposo-m/AxisForge-Shaft-Bearing-Gear-System-Analysis-Platform` (shafts, rolling bearings, parallel-axis gears incl. an FE shaft solver; Python; 1 star; updated Nov 2026). No established package. — [GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=ISO+281+bearing+life+python&type=repositories) [GH-read]
- ISO 6336 gears: `python-gearbox` (LGPL; AGMA 2101-D04 and ISO 6336 stress calculation for spur/helical gears) exists on PyPI as 0.1.x dev builds with "documentation not ready"; maintenance state doubtful. — [PyPI python-gearbox](https://pypi.org/project/python-gearbox/0.1.2.dev/), [GitHub fork](https://github.com/mjfwest/python-gearbox) [search]
- Printed-part anisotropy: literature treats FDM parts as transversely isotropic or orthotropic; interlayer-bond anisotropy is more pronounced than in-layer; Z strength depends on layer adhesion; non-peer-reviewed vendor figure of ">55%" Z-strength loss and a study figure of "up to 60% anisotropy in tensile strength"; guidance is to characterise your own coupons; a Clemson thesis notes FEA "may not be able to effectively predict the behavior of FDM parts in their entirety". — [DoITPoMS FDM](https://www.doitpoms.ac.uk/tlplib/add_manuf/fdm.php), [PADT: constitutive modelling of FDM parts](https://www.padtinc.com/2016/01/04/constitutive-modeling-of-3d-printed-fdm-parts/), [Clemson thesis](https://cecas.clemson.edu/cedar/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Baikerikar_03_17_2017.pdf) [search]
- text-to-cad assumes "isotropic and linear below yield" for all materials — i.e. printed-part anisotropy is not handled in the reference open skill. — [SKILL.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/SKILL.md) [GH-read]

### Inferences
- For a printer/CNC gantry the highest-value verifiers beyond static stress are: (1) static stiffness at the tool point (displacement check at a named face/point under unit loads in X/Y/Z, reported as N/µm), and (2) first natural frequencies of the bonded frame with moving masses lumped — both linear, deterministic and cheap. Treat predicted frequencies as optimistic upper bounds (bonded joints, no belt/bearing compliance), consistent with the i3 Mega study where FEA missed low-frequency modes dominated by compliant elements.
- Belt, rail-carriage and bearing compliances are better added as spring elements with catalogue stiffness than by meshing those parts; rail/bearing life (ISO 281 L10 = (C/P)^p) and bolt checks are closed-form and best implemented as small tested Python functions fed by FEA reaction/interface forces.
- For FDM parts in an isotropic linear solver, the pragmatic guard is a conservative allowable (Z-direction strength with knock-down) plus a rule that the agent states print orientation and checks the principal tensile stress direction against the layer normal; for sustained loads on PLA/PETG a creep/temperature derating must be applied outside the FEA.

### Gaps
- No sourced material was gathered here on thermal verification (heated bed, hot-end, enclosure) with CalculiX/Elmer, on simple CFD for cooling, on fatigue post-processing, or on plastics creep data — these remain open and need a separate pass.
- No authoritative knock-down factors for FDM Z-strength or creep for PLA/PETG were found (only vendor/secondary figures).
- Quality/correctness of the small VDI 2230 / ISO 281 repos was not assessed; none can be recommended as validated.

## 6. Post-processing feedback to the agent and human

### Takeaway
Best documented practice is to give the agent typed numeric findings with locations and built-in singularity heuristics (nodal vs integration-point peaks, peaks at fixtures/bond edges, percentile stress, refinement non-convergence) and to give images as a secondary channel; there is no direct evidence that VLMs read stress contour values reliably, and adjacent evidence (gauge reading ~30% accuracy) argues against relying on it.

### Cited Findings
- text-to-cad singularity/quality heuristics [GH-read]: two von Mises peaks are reported — nodal (extrapolated/averaged; used for safety factor) and integration-point (raw); if the integration-point peak exceeds the nodal peak by more than 50% a `peak_concentration` finding is raised; warnings exist for peaks at fixtures or bonded edges, `mesh_not_converged` (>10% change on refinement), `large_displacement` (>1% of size), `low_margin`, overlapping/closed-gap parts. Guidance: singularities at rigid fixtures, sharp inside corners and point loads grow with refinement — "Report the value a short distance away and suggest a fillet or softer fixture if the location is real". — [linear-static.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/linear-static.md), [SKILL.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/SKILL.md)
- text-to-cad result payload [GH-read]: findings carry `severity`, `code`, `message`, `location`, `hint`; displacement checks can be scoped to listed faces with a label (e.g. "Tip sag"); per-part peak stress/SF/displacement and `weakest_part_peak_at_mm` for assemblies; `result.vtu` (displacement, von_mises, part id) readable with meshio/PyVista; `result.glb` with per-vertex `_VON_MISES`, `_DISPLACEMENT`, `_FACE`, `_PART`; PNG stills with colour bars, skipped with a `solver_note` when no display is available; `deformation_scale: "auto"` scales max displacement to 5% of part size and the skill tells the agent to note that deformation is exaggerated. — [study-file.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/study-file.md)
- CADEngBench-P uses the 95th-percentile von Mises rather than the maximum as its stress comparison quantity, plus max displacement and normalised compliance / stress concentration. — [arXiv 2608.09296 v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09296v1) [search]
- Physics-in-the-Loop feeds back a scalar SF vs band plus named stress hotspots / over-engineered regions in text. — [arXiv 2605.19717 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19717v1) [search]
- Hephaestus-CCX: compact CCX text report per requirement; separate rich-view (multi-view render) image judge improved GPT mean requirement pass by ~6.3 points — the images are renders of geometry for inspection, not shown to be stress plots. — [arXiv 2605.17448 v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17448v1) [search]
- VLM reading reliability (adjacent evidence): MeasureBench (CVPR 2026) — best model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) only 30.2% accuracy reading real measuring instruments, 26.3% synthetic; units recognised well, numeric value reading is the bottleneck. A 2026 scientific-visualisation literacy benchmark finds errors where answers need careful visual estimation (e.g. position relative to contour lines); a systematic VLM evaluation found image input did not consistently beat numeric input. Rainbow colour maps common in FEA are poorly suited to representing stress distributions. — [MeasureBench arXiv 2510.26865](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26865), [arXiv 2607.15176](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15176), [arXiv 2604.24589](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24589), [PMC8596014 colour maps for FEA](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8596014/) [search]
- Stress linearisation and point/segment extraction are available in the open FreeCAD4ParametricFEA scripts; pygccx provides section force/moment post-processing and stress-invariant tools. — [FreeCAD4ParametricFEA](https://github.com/Philippe-AMUZUGA/FreeCAD4ParametricFEA) [search], [pygccx](https://github.com/calculix/pygccx) [GH-read]

### Inferences
- Feedback contract to adopt: JSON with (a) per-requirement value/limit/ratio/status/where, (b) top-N stress hot spots as clusters with xyz, nearest named face/part, and a flag {at_fixture, at_bond_edge, at_reentrant_edge, converging?}, (c) displacement at named probe points (tool point), (d) reaction balance and applied-area checks, (e) mesh stats and convergence delta. Use numeric fields for decisions; attach contour PNGs (perceptually uniform colour map, fixed annotated scale, hot-spot markers with printed values) for the human and for coarse "where" localisation only.
- A robust "design stress" is better taken as percentile or as peak excluding a band of elements adjacent to constraints/ties/sharp edges, reported alongside the raw peak so nothing is hidden.
- Images are demonstrably useful for catching geometry errors (wrong topology, missing holes); that is a geometry-verifier role, distinct from FEA readout.

### Gaps
- No benchmark was found that tests VLMs specifically on reading FEA contour plots; evidence is indirect.
- No sources gathered on strain-energy/ZZ error estimators in CalculiX output (the solver has an error-estimator output variable, but no citation was retrieved here), nor on automatic report generation tooling or PyVista headless rendering specifics (text-to-cad notes PNGs are skipped when no display is available, implying off-screen rendering needs explicit setup).

## 7. Trust: validation suites, regression testing, pitfalls, guards against "plausible but wrong"

### Takeaway
Published agents guard against plausible-but-wrong mainly by taking the solver deck out of the LLM's hands, validating the declarative study strictly before meshing, checking equilibrium and connectivity, auto-refining near low margins, comparing against hand estimates or a matched reference solve, and grading with typed checkers; none of them publishes a NAFEMS-style validation suite for their harness, so a team must build its own regression set.

### Cited Findings
- Guards in text-to-cad [GH-read]: strict schema ("unknown key ... refused with an error naming the valid keys"; `name`-only material refused — E, nu and yield are required); fixed unit system (mm, N, MPa, t/mm³, g) encoded in key names (`vector_N`, `pressure_MPa`, `E_MPa`, `density_t_per_mm3`, `limit_mm`); refusal of unconstrained parts, unreachable loads, and solves whose reactions do not balance applied load; `margin` < 1 refused; atomic output (failed run writes nothing; stale files removed); solver version pinned and recorded in `mesh.solver` ("calculix 2.23 (C3D10)"); single thread for repeatability; inverted quadratic elements are straightened and counted (`mesh.straightened`), a zero-volume element aborts; solve never silently simplified to fit size. — [study-file.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/study-file.md), [linear-static.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/linear-static.md)
- Hand-calc cross-check rule [GH-read]: within ~20% of a beam/Kt≈3/p·r/t estimate is "likely correct"; otherwise investigate reactions, face selection, material in that order. — [linear-static.md](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/fea/references/linear-static.md)
- Matched-reference grading (CADEngBench-P): compare generated vs reference part under identical loads/BCs with log-ratio tolerances, separately reporting whether FEA could be run at all ("reach") vs agreed ("pair pass"). — [arXiv 2608.09296 v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09296v1) [search]
- Deterministic tool the agents "may inspect but cannot modify" (Physics-in-the-Loop); "structured instructions and intermediate checking to reduce formulation errors" (TO-Master); JSON spec -> deck translation (FeaGPT). — [arXiv 2605.19717 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19717v1), [arXiv 2607.01812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01812), [arXiv 2510.21993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21993) [search]
- Element choice: the CalculiX manual says of C3D4, "it is not suited for structural calculations unless a lot of them are used (the element is too stiff). Please use the 10-node tetrahedral element instead", and its golden rules say use quadratic elements (C3D10, C3D15, C3D20(R), ...) except for explicit dynamics. Every 2026 reference kit (text-to-cad, CADEngBench-P) uses C3D10. — [CCX manual, element docs](https://web.mit.edu/calculix_v2.7/CalculiX/ccx_2.7/doc/ccx/node32.html), [CCX 2.22 manual](https://www.dhondt.de/ccx_2.22.pdf) [search]
- Units: CalculiX is unitless — "it's the user's responsibility to ensure that the same unit system is used consistently"; the mm-N-s-tonne-MPa-mJ set gives steel density 7.85e-9 t/mm³. — [CalculiX discourse: units](https://calculix.discourse.group/t/quantity-and-unit-conversion-in-ccx/299), [FreeCAD FEM CalculiX wiki](https://wiki.freecad.org/FEM_CalculiX) [search]
- NAFEMS benchmarks: a public cross-code LE10 (thick plate under pressure) study from the FeenoX project includes CalculiX among compared solvers, with scripts on GitHub and stress-vs-refinement plots; FeenoX reproduces LE1 (σy at D ≈ 92.70 vs 92.7 reference). No ready-made public "NAFEMS suite for CalculiX" (LE1/LE10/FV32 with pass/fail) was found. — [seamplex LE10 README](https://www.seamplex.com/feenox/tests/nafems/le10/README.pdf), [FeenoX mechanical examples](https://www.seamplex.com/feenox/examples/mechanical.pdf) [search]
- "Valid STEP" and agent self-reported success are not acceptance criteria: independent audit of a commercial CAD agent on H-CCX/MUSE/CADGenBench prompts found valid solids with interferences, wrong thickness (14 mm vs 50 mm), unchanged blade counts, and a claimed-validated design failing its dowel contract. — [QiuYi111/Reify issue #37](https://github.com/QiuYi111/Reify/issues/37) [GH-read]
- Bolt-pretension-on-tets silently losing most of the preload is an example of a solver-specific trap that produces a converged, plausible, wrong answer unless a section-force print is checked. — [PrePoLix PR #208](https://github.com/KwentiN-ui/PrePoLix/pull/208) [search]

### Inferences
- Harness regression suite to build (synthesis): (1) closed-form cases through the full CAD->mesh->solve->extract path (cantilever tip deflection and root stress, plate with hole Kt, thick cylinder under pressure, self-weight reaction, first bending frequency of a beam) asserted within tolerance at default mesh; (2) NAFEMS LE1/LE10 (static) and FV32/FV52-type (modal) with their published targets; (3) unit-system canaries (frequency of a steel beam catches a 1000x density error because f ∝ sqrt(E/ρ) shifts by ~31.6x); (4) negative tests that must be refused — floating part, load on fixed face, mirrored-face ambiguity, zero-area selection, unbalanced reactions; (5) assembly cases — two bonded blocks of different materials vs analytic series stiffness, and a free-free modal count of exactly six zero modes; (6) pin solver/mesher versions and store golden `result.json` for drift detection on upgrades.
- Runtime invariants on every loop iteration: mass from mesh vs CAD volume x density; Σreactions = −Σapplied (force and moment); applied area vs CAD face area; min scaled Jacobian / straightened-element count; refinement delta; hand-calc bracket where a template applies; reject if any fail rather than report numbers.
- Keep "could not verify" as a first-class outcome (CADEngBench's reach vs pass split): an FEA that failed to mesh or was refused must never be reported as either pass or fail of the design requirement.

### Gaps
- No public, maintained NAFEMS (LE1/LE10/FV32) pass/fail suite specifically for CalculiX + Gmsh was found; the FeenoX LE10 comparison is the nearest open artefact and its numeric table was not read.
- None of the surveyed agent papers was confirmed (from extracts) to report validation of its FEA kit against analytical/NAFEMS references; Hephaestus-CCX's kit validation details could not be read.
- Over-constraint pitfalls (thermal/Poisson locking from fully clamped faces, redundant MPCs with ties) are covered here only by text-to-cad's statement that rigid fixtures overstate nearby stress; no deeper sourced treatment was collected.
