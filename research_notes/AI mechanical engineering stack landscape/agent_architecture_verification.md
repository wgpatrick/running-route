# Agent architectures, feedback loops, verification and evaluation for LLM agents doing end-to-end mechanical engineering (state as of Nov 2026)

Research-access note for the report writer: in this session arxiv.org, huggingface.co, epoch.ai and substack.com were unreachable (network allowlist). Paper facts below therefore come from search-result summaries of the arXiv/proceedings pages or from secondary pages (GitHub, Anthropic blog), not from reading the full papers. Every number that I could not confirm against a primary page is flagged "(secondary, unverified)". Pages read directly: anthropic.com engineering posts, GitHub READMEs (build123d-mcp, agentcad, text-to-cad, cad-skill, cadgenbench) and the torno issue #42 landscape survey.

## 1. What agent architectures have been tried for engineering design, and what results / failure modes were reported?

### Takeaway
The published pattern has converged on "LLM writes code-CAD (CadQuery/build123d) + executes + gets structured feedback from a reviewer", with role-split multi-agent variants (planner / CAD engineer / geometry reviewer / structural reviewer / DFM reviewer / optimizer). Reported gains from the multi-agent split are modest and self-reported; the consistent wins come from (a) a verifier that is not the generator, (b) feedback grounded in kernel/solver output rather than opinion, and (c) small verified steps instead of single-shot generation.

### Cited Findings
- CADCodeVerify (Alrashedy et al., Georgia Tech, arXiv 2410.05340; 2024/2025): a VLM generates validation questions about a rendered object, answers them from the render, and feeds corrections back into CadQuery code. With GPT-4 it reported a 7.30% reduction in point-cloud distance and a ~5.5% compile/success-rate improvement over prior refinement work (the figure is given as 5.0% on one page, i.e. differs across versions). Compile rates on CADPrompt: GPT-4 96.5%, Gemini 85%, CodeLlama 73.5%. Ablation: removing few-shot examples raised point-cloud distance 0.126 -> 0.141; removing reference images -> 0.153. — [arXiv 2410.05340 (search summary)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05340v1)
- The same paper's self-QA step is weak: the model answered questions about its own render correctly only 65-68% of the time (secondary, unverified against the paper). — [torno issue #42 survey](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42)
- Query2CAD: iterative refinement added +23.1 points over a 53.6% first-attempt success, most of the gain in the first refinement round (secondary, unverified). — [torno issue #42 survey](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42)
- Physics-in-the-Loop (arXiv 2605.19717, IJCAI 2026): LangGraph multi-agent "Generate-Simulate-Refine" loop with Planner, CAD Engineer, Geometry Reviewer and Structural Reviewer; the Structural Reviewer runs FEA and returns feedback to the CAD Engineer. Abstract claims a "4.2 increase in structural complexity" and +3.5% compile rate vs. similar agentic methods, plus a new benchmark for functional validity. Authors' own numbers; no independent replication found. — [IJCAI 2026 proceedings](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/714); [arXiv 2605.19717](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19717)
- Same paper, model comparison: Gemini 3 Flash "significantly outperformed all models" at 95.6% execution and 84.4% FEA success (secondary search summary). — [arXiv 2605.19717](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19717)
- MDO agent (arXiv 2511.17511, 2025): Modeler agent turns text into parametric CadQuery; Verifier agent runs FEA in Ansys Mechanical; separate Optimizer agent runs structural optimization. Reported ~70% of the FEA workflow automated and 100% optimization-code generation success (authors' numbers). — [arXiv 2511.17511](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.17511)
- DFM-Redesign (Welbeck et al., Northwestern, arXiv 2609.05559, Sept 2026): training-free multi-agent pipeline; a multimodal "DFM Reviewer" proposes one intent-preserving CNC-manufacturability improvement at a time, a "CAD Modifier" applies it as an edit to the CadQuery program, and each transition is verified before the next. Motivation stated: single-shot CAD generation degrades as part complexity grows. The paper has a section headed "Manufacturability improvement is not yet quantified". — [arXiv 2609.05559](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05559v1)
- AgentsCAD (George et al., arXiv 2607.02448, 2026): FDM design-for-manufacturing; parses STEP B-Rep, detects overhangs above 45 degrees deterministically, builds a face-adjacency graph, then a blackboard-style multi-agent layer (reported as Claude Sonnet) proposes reorientation/fillet/chamfer fixes with a CAD-code verifier agent checking the reasoner; optional GraphSAGE feature labels. — [arXiv 2607.02448](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02448v2); [third-party review](https://github.com/reacher-z/Awesome-CUA/issues/2808)
- COSMO-Agent (arXiv 2604.05547, 2026): closed CAD-CAE loop where an 8B LLM trained with GRPO on rollout-log rewards learns to edit parameters across rounds (RL-trained small model rather than a frontier model in a harness). — [arXiv 2604.05547](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05547)
- MIT DeCoDE: "AI Agents in Engineering Design: A Multi-Agent Framework for Aesthetic and Aerodynamic Car Design" (Elrefaie, Qian, Wu, Chen, Dai, Ahmed; ASME IDETC DAC 2025) introduces "Design Agents" integrated into the automotive workflow. — [DeCoDE publications](https://decode.mit.edu/publications/)
- MIT DeCoDE: "From Concept to Manufacturing: Evaluating Vision-Language Models for Engineering Design" (Picard et al.; arXiv Nov 2023, Artificial Intelligence Review 2025) tested GPT-4V and LLaVA 1.6 34B across conceptual design, system/detailed design, manufacturing/inspection and education (sketch similarity, CAD generation, topology optimization, manufacturability, textbook problems; >1,000 queries released); it documents proficiency plus limitations, notably spatial reasoning. — [arXiv 2311.12668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12668v3); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-025-11290-y)
- Other named systems: "From Idea to CAD" multi-agent collaborative design (arXiv 2503.04417, 2025); ArtiCAD articulated assemblies via multi-agent code generation (arXiv 2604.10992); CADDesigner general-purpose agent for conceptual CAD (arXiv 2508.01031); "LLM-enabled Multi-Agent Autonomous Mechatronics Design Framework" (arXiv 2504.14681; a "78% reduction in design iteration time" appears only in an aggregator summary, unverified); TO-Agents multi-agent preference-guided topology optimization (arXiv 2605.21622); "Large language model agent as a mechanical designer" (J. Engineering Design, 2026, seen only as a citation). — [search result set](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.04417); [ArtiCAD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10992); [CADDesigner](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.01031); [mechatronics](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.14681); [TO-Agents](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.21622)
- Practitioner/open-source harnesses (2026) all implement the same single-agent loop: "the agent writes build123d or CadQuery, a local tool builds, measures, renders and shows it". Largest: earthtojake/text-to-cad (MIT, ~22k stars, created 2026-04-22), Amagine3D (~6.3k), CADAM (OpenSCAD, ~5.3k), Multi-Agent-CAD (~1k), cad-skill (690), agentcad (169), build123d-mcp (125). — [torno issue #42](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42)
- Silent failure is a named failure mode: in one six-agent test, 9 of 16 failures went unreported by the tool (secondary, context truncated). — [torno issue #42](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42)
- Anthropic's harness evidence (software, not CAD, but directly transferable): generator agents "reliably skew positive when grading their own work"; a separate evaluator is far easier to tune toward skepticism; early evaluators found real issues then "talked themselves into approving". Solo Opus 4.5 run: 20 min, $9, broken core; planner+generator+evaluator harness: 6 h, $200, far more functional (>20x cost). With Opus 4.6 the sprint decomposition was removed, the planner kept (without it the generator under-scoped), and the evaluator moved to a single end pass (~3 h 50 min, $124.70). Lesson: every harness component encodes an assumption about what the model cannot do, and those go stale. — [Anthropic, Harness design for long-running application development, 2026-03-24](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)

### Inferences
- For a small team on Claude, the evidence favours: one main agent (code-CAD in sandbox) + one independent reviewer subagent with fresh context and access to deterministic checks, plus an explicit up-front spec/plan step. Heavier role-play teams (requirements engineer / designer / analyst / reviewer as peers) are common in papers, but none of the numbers found isolate a benefit of the role split from the benefit of adding tool-grounded feedback.
- The DFM-Redesign "one verified edit at a time" and build123d-mcp "one feature at a time, measure after major steps" advice are the same pattern; it should be encoded in the skill/prompt and enforced by tooling (snapshots, diffs).
- Because model upgrades invalidate harness assumptions, the harness should be built to be ablated (remove one component at a time and re-run the eval suite), per Anthropic's method.

### Gaps
- Could not read full papers (arxiv blocked): no verified details for MechAgents (Ni & Buehler), CAD-Assistant, 3D-PreMise, Idea2CAD internals, the DeCoDE truss/linkage LLM-agent papers, or DeCoDE car-agent quantitative results. From memory (unverified, not sourced here): MechAgents used AutoGen-style planner/coder/critic teams solving FEniCS elasticity problems; 3D-PreMise was an early GPT-4 visual-feedback CAD program loop. Treat these as leads only.
- No controlled single-agent vs multi-agent ablation on the same mechanical task with a 2026 frontier model was found.
- "4.2 increase in structural complexity" in Physics-in-the-Loop: unit/definition not available from the summary.

## 2. Visual and geometric feedback: how effective is render-and-critique vs programmatic geometric checks? VLM spatial limits in 2025-2026

### Takeaway
The strongest 2026 evidence says kernel-level (B-rep) executable checks beat VLM judgment by a wide margin as a verifier (0.938 vs 0.474 agreement with humans, secondary) and also work as feedback (+~10 points pass rate). Renders remain useful for gross-error detection and for the human, but frontier VLMs still show view-dependent spatial failures, so renders should be a secondary signal with multi-view tiles, scale bars and labels, never the acceptance gate.

### Cited Findings
- CADTests / CADTestBench (Mallis et al., arXiv 2605.07807, May 2026): executable software tests that verify a generated CAD model against the prompt's design specification, written with selectors and B-Rep inspection primitives: topology counts, bounding-box dimensions, areas and volumes, centre-of-mass queries, containment tests, line-to-geometry intersections. Built on the 200 CADPrompt programs; 5,937 individual tests. — [arXiv 2605.07807](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07807v1); [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.07807)
- CADTests reported: B-rep checks agreed with human judgment 0.938 vs 0.474 for a vision-model metric; using the tests as feedback raised pass rate from 0.715 to 0.810 (secondary, not verified against the paper). — [torno issue #42](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42)
- BenchCAD: models scored 0.82-0.84 on code/text CAD understanding but only 0.46-0.59 on image understanding (secondary, unverified). — [torno issue #42](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42)
- Blueprint2CAD (arXiv 2611.02433, Nov 2026): tests whether a VLM can tell if an orthographic drawing and a shaded render show the same part. Claude's accuracy falls from 0.98 on right-side renders to 0.46 on top renders without dimensions; most models are weakest on top and bottom views; models lean on a label prior that chain-of-thought shifts but does not remove. — [arXiv 2611.02433](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02433)
- OmniCAD (arXiv 2608.22637, Aug 2026): 3D spatial reasoning benchmark over mechanical/robotic assemblies, including a tool-augmented setting where the model iteratively chooses viewpoints, inspects and refines. — [arXiv 2608.22637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22637)
- Other 2026 drawing-literacy evidence: general VLMs reach only 33.03-37.88% semantic accuracy on architectural floor-plan CAD drawings (ArchPlanVQA); AECV-Bench reports a gradient from OCR (good) to spatial reasoning to symbol-centric understanding (worst). — [ArchPlanVQA](https://doi.org/10.1061/JCCEE5.CPENG-7571); [AECV-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04819)
- "Large Language Models and their Awareness of Mechanics and Spatial Geometry" (arXiv 2608.14615, Aug 2026) exists as a direct study of this question; content not retrieved. — [arXiv 2608.14615](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14615)
- DesignQA (MIT DeCoDE; JCISE Feb 2025): models tested (GPT-4o, GPT-4, Gemini-1.0, Claude-Opus, LLaVA-1.5) struggled with recognising technical components in CAD images and analysing engineering drawings, in addition to rule extraction. — [arXiv 2404.07917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07917)
- CADCodeVerify ablation: giving the VLM reference images matters (point-cloud distance 0.126 with vs 0.153 without); on harder objects it was the only refinement method that improved compile rate (~+9% at first refinement). — [arXiv 2410.05340](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05340v2)
- Tooling that implements programmatic feedback today: build123d-mcp exposes render (PNG/SVG/DXF), measurement (volume, area, bounding boxes, topology, centre of mass), `find_candidates(kind="hole", ...)` feature recognition, `interface_features()`, `edit_feature()` that reports predicted vs measured volume change and refuses unsafe edits, `compare(kind="shape")`, `validate()` before export, session snapshots, and printability / fit-alignment comparisons. — [pzfreo/build123d-mcp](https://github.com/pzfreo/build123d-mcp)
- Self-reported effect of that tooling: on CADGenBench (June 2026) the same model went 0.360 -> 0.457 and validity 88% -> 100% with build123d-mcp; a separate write-up reports a much smaller gain (0.6268 -> 0.6391). Both self-reported and mutually inconsistent in scale. — [pzfreo/build123d-mcp](https://github.com/pzfreo/build123d-mcp); [Experiments in 3D substack (not readable here)](https://experimentsin3d.substack.com/p/i-asked-claude-what-would-help-it)
- agentcad returns JSON with dimensions, edge lengths, face areas, circular/cylindrical feature measurements, topology, orientation, addressable geometry IDs and "edit risk"; `diff` reports shared / reference-only / candidate-only volume between versions; `--preview` gives a four-view PNG (top, front, right, iso). — [jdilla1277/agentcad](https://github.com/jdilla1277/agentcad)
- cad-render-mcp returns several views tiled in one image with a mm scale bar, inline in the tool result. — [Willianvdv/cad-render-mcp](https://github.com/Willianvdv/cad-render-mcp)
- Image cost: a 1280 px image is roughly 1,600 tokens (secondary). — [torno issue #42](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42)
- cad-skill embeds assertions in the generated script itself (e.g. two cable-tray segments must neither collide nor rattle), a `mating.py` fit-check helper, build-volume checks, and a `--strict` watertightness failure on previews. — [flowful-ai/cad-skill](https://github.com/flowful-ai/cad-skill)
- CADGenBench scores "interface match" with authored keep-in and keep-out sub-volumes and topology via Betti numbers (b0, b1, b2), i.e. functional geometric assertions rather than pure similarity. — [huggingface/cadgenbench](https://github.com/huggingface/cadgenbench)

### Inferences
- Recommended hierarchy of feedback for the harness: (1) kernel validity (`validate`, watertight, no self-intersection); (2) spec-derived geometric assertions ("unit tests for geometry": bbox, volume/mass, hole count/diameter/position, keep-in/keep-out volumes, interference and clearance between mating parts, min wall thickness); (3) solver results (FoS, displacement); (4) multi-view renders for gross-shape sanity, with the reviewer told which view shows what; (5) human view.
- Because top/bottom views are the weakest for VLMs, standard render tiles should favour iso + labelled orthographic views with dimension/scale overlays, and section views should be paired with a numeric query (wall thickness value) rather than left to visual estimate. This is an inference from Blueprint2CAD, not a tested result.
- Writing the geometric tests before the CAD (from the requirement) mirrors CADTests and Anthropic's "sprint contract" idea and prevents the generator from grading itself.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study found on section views, exploded views or annotated screenshots specifically as VLM feedback for CAD agents.
- No verified numbers for the latest Claude models on spatial benchmarks beyond the Blueprint2CAD summary line.
- Minimum-wall-thickness and interference tooling: found as capabilities in harnesses, but no accuracy evaluation of these checks.

## 3. Requirements and specification: how are requirements, loads, interfaces, materials, tolerances represented so results are verifiable? Units, materials, standard parts, knowledge retrieval

### Takeaway
The verifiable-spec practice that has evidence behind it is (a) clarify missing dimensions before generating, and (b) hold the spec as a machine-checkable artifact (spec.json / executable tests / keep-in-keep-out volumes) evaluated separately from geometry validity and from human approval. SysML v2 + LLM work exists but reports that LLM traceability outputs are unreliable without parsers and deterministic validation.

### Cited Findings
- ProCAD (arXiv 2602.03045, 2026): asking the user for missing dimensions first cut mean Chamfer distance by 79.9% (secondary, unverified). — [torno issue #42](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42)
- agentcad has a first-class "Specify" stage: `check-spec` evaluates a `spec.json` against a STEP file; the README states the spec check is distinct from geometry validity and from human approval; enforced workflow Inspect -> Specify -> Build -> Validate -> Deliver, where a failed check sends the agent back to the earliest wrong stage. — [jdilla1277/agentcad](https://github.com/jdilla1277/agentcad)
- CADTests turn the prompt's design specification into executable tests (5,937 tests over 200 parts, i.e. ~30 assertions per part). — [arXiv 2605.07807](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07807v1)
- MecSOAgent (ASME J. Mech. Design, 2026): multi-agent framework using LLMs plus a knowledge graph to turn natural-language requirements into optimization scripts and SysML 2.0 physical system models for conceptual mechanical design. — [ASME JMD](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/mechanicaldesign/article-abstract/doi/10.1115/1.4072148/1233727/A-Multi-Agent-Architecture-of-Requirement-Driven?redirectedFrom=fulltext)
- SysTemp (arXiv 2506.21608, 2025): multi-agent SysML v2 generation needs a SysML-v2-specific parser agent for syntactic/structural validation and template-based generation. — [arXiv 2506.21608](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.21608)
- A standards-aligned (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018) multi-agent semantic validation framework for SysML v2 notes that LLMs lack inherent knowledge of the SysML v2 specification, which often makes their traceability outputs unreliable; it uses a deterministic pipeline. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0920548926000656)
- MDPI AI-journal study: LLM-assisted SysML v2 generation requires structured prompting, explicit validation and task-specific evaluation. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-2688/7/8/274)
- "Two Truths and A Lie?" (arXiv 2609.03230, 2026) benchmarks off-the-shelf LLMs for requirements quality assessment including false alarms and misses (findings not retrieved). — [arXiv 2609.03230](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03230)
- DesignQA shows requirement/rule documents are themselves a failure point: on the 140-page Formula SAE rulebook (1,451 questions; rule extraction, comprehension, compliance) even leading models struggled with precise rule identification, returning nearby rules or wrongly including child rules. — [arXiv 2404.07917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07917); [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/169000)
- Interfaces: build123d-mcp's `interface_features()` surfaces flat faces carrying holes so the user can mark which holes to protect; `edit_feature` takes `protected_refs`; CADGenBench scores mating-feature correctness with keep-in/keep-out volumes. — [build123d-mcp](https://github.com/pzfreo/build123d-mcp); [cadgenbench](https://github.com/huggingface/cadgenbench)
- Domain knowledge is shipped as skills/helper libraries rather than retrieved ad hoc: text-to-cad bundles separate SKILL.md folders for CAD, drawings, FEA (CalculiX: peak stress, safety factor, displacement), manufacturing-process checks (machined, bent, moulded, printed), fabrication services (SendCutSend DXF/STEP validation), 3D printing; cad-skill vendors `gridfinity.py` and `mating.py` next to generated scripts. — [earthtojake/text-to-cad](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad); [flowful-ai/cad-skill](https://github.com/flowful-ai/cad-skill)

### Inferences
- A practical spec schema for the harness: requirements with IDs -> each mapped to >=1 executable check (geometric assertion, solver threshold with load case/material/FoS target, DFM rule, mass/cost bound) -> results table in the report. This gives traceability without full SysML v2; SysML v2 export can be a later interoperability layer since LLM fluency in it is still reported as weak.
- An explicit "clarify under-specified dimensions/loads/interfaces" step is probably the single highest-leverage requirements behaviour (ProCAD evidence), and should be an eval dimension (does the agent ask when it should?).
- Units: no source found evaluating pint-style unit enforcement in agents; recommending it (typed quantities in the spec and calc sheets, mm-only in geometry) is opinion based on general practice.

### Gaps
- No sources retrieved on: pint/unit handling in LLM engineering agents; material databases; bd_warehouse / cq_warehouse / McMaster part libraries in agent harnesses; retrieval over Shigley / Roark / Machinery's Handbook; ISO/ASME paywall and licensing constraints for agent access to standards. These key-question items remain unanswered by evidence in this pass.
- Tolerances/GD&T: no evidence found of agents representing or verifying tolerances beyond build123d-mcp's 2D drawing previews and fit checks.
- "Design-by-contract for CAD" as a named approach: no paper found; CADTests and script-embedded assertions are the closest.

## 4. Tool-surface design for Claude: code-execution-first vs fine-grained MCP tools; Anthropic guidance; existing CAD MCP servers; human viewer feedback

### Takeaway
Both Anthropic guidance and every successful 2026 CAD harness point to code-execution-first: the agent writes a build123d/CadQuery script in a sandbox, and a small set of high-level, token-bounded tools/CLI commands (run, inspect/measure, render, check-spec, diff, validate, export) return structured JSON plus saved artifacts. Domain knowledge goes in Skills (progressive disclosure), verification in an independent subagent, and the human gets a live local web viewer with version compare and pinned comments.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic, "Code execution with MCP" (2025-11-04): presenting tools as code/filesystem so the agent loads only needed definitions and filters data in the execution environment cut one workflow from 150,000 to 2,000 tokens (98.7%); intermediate results stay out of context; loops/conditionals run in code; state persisted to files enables resumption; working code can be saved as reusable functions and wrapped with SKILL.md. Caveat: requires secure sandboxing, resource limits and monitoring. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/code-execution-with-mcp)
- Anthropic, "Writing tools for agents" (2025-09-11): build a few tools for high-impact workflows rather than wrapping every API endpoint; consolidate frequently chained operations; namespace tools; return human-meaningful context rather than opaque IDs; offer `response_format` concise/detailed (example 72 vs 206 tokens); paginate/filter/truncate (Claude Code caps tool responses at 25,000 tokens by default); make errors actionable; develop tools eval-first with realistic multi-call tasks, read transcripts, use held-out sets, and let Claude Code refactor the tools from transcripts. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents)
- Anthropic harness post (2026-03-24): planner -> generator -> evaluator; agents communicate through files; generator and evaluator negotiate a "contract" of testable criteria before building (one sprint had 27 criteria); evaluator grades each criterion against hard thresholds; with Opus 4.5+ context resets were dropped in favour of automatic compaction in one session; with Opus 4.6 sprints were removed. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- agentcad design rules (as stated by the project): final result of every command is JSON on stdout, progress on stderr; failures structured with an `error_kind`; import/inspect responses bounded to ~2,000 tokens with saved artifacts and retrieval actions for the rest; idempotency keys make retries safe; one script and one CAD API (mixed runtimes rejected); every build is versioned; build123d default, CadQuery compatibility mode only. Its own evidence caveat: a single GPT-5 Nano release-candidate run (0.245 overall, 80/81 valid) is explicitly not an evaluation. — [jdilla1277/agentcad](https://github.com/jdilla1277/agentcad)
- build123d-mcp tool surface: `execute()` against a persistent CAD session, `last_error` resource, render / measure / import-export capabilities, feature-level tools (`find_candidates`, `interface_features`, `edit_feature`), `compare`, `validate`, snapshots, and `install_skill(target, skill)` that installs modeling / drawing / repair workflow guidance for Claude, Cursor, Windsurf, or AGENTS.md. Recommended loop: one feature at a time, render or measure after major steps, validate before export; on failure inspect `last_error` and retry a smaller step. stdio gives one isolated session per process; HTTP mode shares one session and has no auth. — [pzfreo/build123d-mcp](https://github.com/pzfreo/build123d-mcp)
- text-to-cad packaging: per-app plugin manifests (Claude, Codex, Cursor, Grok, Gemini) bundling skills + an MCP server + a `cad` CLI (`cad doctor --check`); models are build123d/OpenCASCADE Python scripts; outputs STEP, STL, 3MF, GLB, DXF profiles and flat patterns; Blender for final renders after a quick draft. — [earthtojake/text-to-cad](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad)
- cad-skill structure: SKILL.md + `design-review.md` visual-inspection/printability checklist + wrapper `run_cadquery_model.py` (runs script in subprocess, captures errors, optional preview, bed-size check, returns JSON so Claude can self-correct) + headless six-view preview; phased workflow requirements -> base shape -> features -> finish. Evidence is anecdotal (printed models). — [flowful-ai/cad-skill](https://github.com/flowful-ai/cad-skill)
- Bridges to incumbent CAD exist as MCP servers/connectors: FreeCAD MCP (neka-nat, ~3k stars), Autodesk Fusion connector for Claude, Onshape FeatureScript MCP (PTC, announced 2026-08-13 via Onshape Labs), community SolidWorks/Plasticity/Shapr3D MCPs, Zoo MCP, Blender MCP (~32k stars). — [torno issue #42](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42)
- Claude Code specifics noted by practitioners (Oct 2026): tool search is on by default and defers MCP tool schemas; 33 hook events exist (e.g. PostToolUse, Stop, SubagentStop, PostCompact, PostToolBatch), with UserPromptSubmit / PostToolUse / Stop used to enforce CAD checks. — [torno issue #42](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42)
- Human viewer patterns: agentcad serves a stable localhost project URL that refreshes after each completed build, keeps the last good model visible after failures, preloads previous + current versions for side-by-side / overlay / part-level change review, supports turntable GIF export, and stores spatial comments pinned to a surface and named part as local JSON that the agent reads and answers through `review` commands. OCP CAD Viewer for VS Code (bernhard-42, Apache-2.0, 418 stars) is the established build123d/CadQuery viewer. — [jdilla1277/agentcad](https://github.com/jdilla1277/agentcad); [torno issue #42](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42)

### Inferences
- Proposed Claude tool surface: Bash + file tools in a sandbox with build123d, a thin `cad` CLI (or 6-10 MCP tools) returning bounded JSON: run/build (versioned), inspect/measure/query, render (tiled views, section option), check-spec (run assertions), interference/clearance, diff, validate, fea-run, dfm-check, export, viewer/review-comments. Fine-grained "create_box / add_fillet" style tools are contraindicated by Anthropic's guidance and absent from the leading harnesses.
- A CLI + Skill is at least as good as MCP for Claude Code (works via Bash, zero schema context cost); MCP is useful mainly for the stateful session (persistent kernel) and for hosts without a shell. Opinion grounded in the code-execution post and tool-search deferral.
- Use hooks (PostToolUse after script edits; Stop) to run validity + spec checks automatically so the agent cannot "finish" with failing assertions - this directly addresses the silent-failure and self-approval findings.
- Pinned 3D review comments readable by the agent are the best-evidenced human-in-the-loop UI primitive found; replicate with any STEP->glTF web viewer.

### Gaps
- No comparative measurement (same model, same tasks) of code-execution-first vs fine-grained CAD tool calls was found; the preference rests on Anthropic's general guidance and ecosystem convergence.
- Anthropic's Agent Skills / subagents / context-management posts and Claude Agent SDK docs were not fetched in this pass; details there (e.g. skill progressive-disclosure limits) are not cited.
- three-cad-viewer, Yet Another CAD Viewer, model-viewer and STEP-to-glTF pipelines: not specifically researched; only OCP CAD Viewer and agentcad's viewer are sourced.

## 5. Evaluation: benchmarks, metrics, and lessons on benchmark limitations

### Takeaway
Shape-similarity metrics (Chamfer/IoU) are the legacy default but correlate poorly with whether a part is right; 2026 benchmarks moved to validity gates + functional checks (executable B-rep tests, interface keep-in/keep-out, topology, simulation-scored rubrics). Frontier models are still far from saturating functional engineering benchmarks: generation from drawings scores below 0.4 on CADGenBench, and no agent fully solved any FEABench problem.

### Cited Findings
- CADPrompt: 200 3D objects with natural-language prompts and expert-written CadQuery code; metrics: compile rate, point-cloud distance, Hausdorff distance, IoGT. — [arXiv 2410.05340](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05340v2)
- CADTestBench: 5,937 executable tests over the CADPrompt parts; test-based verdicts agree with humans far more than a VLM metric (0.938 vs 0.474; secondary). — [arXiv 2605.07807](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07807v1); [torno issue #42](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42)
- CADGenBench (Hugging Face, 2026): tasks are generation of a 3D model from an engineering drawing and editing of an existing STEP file; tool-agnostic (submit one STEP/BREP per sample); scored against private ground truth on validity (gate - failure zeroes everything), shape similarity (surface-distance F1, volume IoU), interface match (keep-in/keep-out volumes), topology (Betti numbers); rows are published unvalidated until maintainer review; ships a reference agent that writes build123d/CadQuery, renders, reviews, iterates. — [huggingface/cadgenbench](https://github.com/huggingface/cadgenbench)
- CADGenBench standings (third-party leaderboard mirror, treat cautiously): "Claude Fable 5 HF Baseline" leads with aggregate 0.451; generation scores stay below 0.4 for all entries; a blog-style summary cites usable geometry in ~82-91% of editing tasks but only 2-10% of generation tasks. — [benchmarklist.com](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/cadgenbench/)
- Harness matters as much as model on that benchmark: 0.360 -> 0.457 for the same model with build123d-mcp (self-reported). — [pzfreo/build123d-mcp](https://github.com/pzfreo/build123d-mcp)
- Epoch AI CadEval: text prompt -> CAD program; reports rendering success, Chamfer/Hausdorff distance and volume/consistency checks. A third-party page lists o3 74%, Gemini 2.5 Pro 64%, o4-mini 62% but appears stale/mislabelled; no reliable 2026 frontier numbers obtained. — [Epoch CadEval](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/cad-eval); [devmesh mirror](https://devmesh.me/models/benchmarks/cad_eval_external)
- EngDesign (NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks; arXiv 2509.16204): design tasks across nine engineering domains, each with human-written task-specific evaluation scripts that verify functional feasibility by simulation (e.g. SPICE, structural FEA), constraint validation or performance tests; partial-credit rubrics 0-100 plus pass/fail and logs; each LLM run three times per task; 10+ models evaluated. — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/664f777548205fb6e0cbb0965e8d2e16-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html); [arXiv 2509.16204](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16204v2)
- FEABench (Mudur et al., Google; arXiv 2504.06260): 15 manually verified COMSOL problems (Gold) + 200 parsed (Large). Best strategy produced executable COMSOL API calls 88% of the time, yet no LLM or agent completely and correctly solved any problem. Name clash: "FEA-Bench" is an unrelated repo-level code benchmark. — [arXiv 2504.06260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06260v1); [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/feabench-evaluating-language-models-on-real-world-physics-reasoning-ability/)
- DesignQA: 1,451 questions on FSAE rules + CAD/document data (rule extraction, comprehension, compliance). — [arXiv 2404.07917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07917)
- Physics-in-the-Loop contributes a benchmark and metrics for functional (load-bearing) validity of generated CAD. — [IJCAI 2026](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/714)
- Other 2026 benchmarks named: CADBench/CadBench multimodal CAD program generation (arXiv 2605.10873), DepthBenchCAD on audit depth vs reliability of conclusions (arXiv 2609.15122), IterCAD iterative program repair from orthographic views (arXiv 2608.24020), Blueprint2CAD, OmniCAD, BenchCAD, Text2CAD-Bench, MUSE. — [CadBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10873); [DepthBenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15122); [IterCAD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24020); [torno issue #42](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42)
- Curated list: awesome-ai4cad (700+ papers, 2018-2026, generation / understanding / optimization / manufacturing). — [gudo7208/awesome-ai4cad](https://github.com/gudo7208/awesome-ai4cad)
- Practitioner test of seven commercial text-to-CAD tools (Xometry/Wevolver) exists as a usefulness review for engineers (content not retrieved). — [Wevolver](https://www.wevolver.com/article/we-tested-7-text-to-cad-tools-are-they-actually-useful-for-engineers)
- Anthropic's eval method for tools/harnesses: realistic tasks needing dozens of tool calls with verifiable outcomes; verifiers must not reject correct answers on formatting; track runtime, tool-call count, tokens, errors; read transcripts; held-out sets. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents)

### Inferences
- Eval suite design for the team: (tier 0) script executes + valid watertight B-rep; (tier 1) per-task executable geometric tests incl. interface keep-in/keep-out; (tier 2) functional: FoS/deflection target met under stated load case in an independent solver run, mass budget, DFM rule pass for the stated process; (tier 3) process behaviour: asked for missing info, reported own failures honestly, calc-sheet numbers reproduce; (tier 4) periodic human expert review and a small number of physical build-and-test tasks. Report pass rate over >=3 runs per task (EngDesign protocol) plus cost/time/tool calls.
- Keep IoU/Chamfer only as a diagnostic where a reference exists; do not optimise the harness against it (CADTests agreement gap).
- The FEABench "88% executable, 0% fully correct" result is the key caution for simulation: executability is not correctness, so FEA evals need reference solutions (analytic beam/plate/pressure-vessel cases, mesh-convergence checks) rather than "ran without error".
- Self-reported harness leaderboard gains vary by an order of magnitude between write-ups; run your own ablations.

### Gaps
- EngDesign per-model pass rates and failure taxonomy not retrieved (arxiv/hf blocked).
- No benchmark found that covers the whole requirements -> CAD -> simulation -> manufacturing chain end to end, nor one with physical build-and-test ground truth.
- Text2CAD eval, CAD-Recode metrics, Text-to-CadQuery evals, FE/PE-exam-style QA results: not retrieved in this pass.
- CadEval current frontier scores: unavailable (epoch.ai blocked; mirror unreliable).

## 6. Optimization loops, human-in-the-loop checkpoints, reliability/safety, provenance and version control

### Takeaway
Published loops put the LLM in charge of setting up the parametric model and the optimization problem while a conventional optimizer or simulator does the numeric search; evidence is thin and mostly vendor- or author-reported. For reliability, the best-supported practices are independent verification, explicit separation of "spec check passed" from "human approved", versioned builds with diffs, and honest failure reporting; liability/sign-off norms for AI-generated engineering are not addressed by the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- MDO agent (arXiv 2511.17511): Modeler (CadQuery) -> Verifier (Ansys FEA) -> Optimizer agent; 100% optimization-code generation success, ~70% of FEA workflow automated (authors' numbers). — [arXiv 2511.17511](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.17511)
- SimScale text-to-CAD write-up (vendor blog): a 16-design Bayesian optimization run in 3 rounds, recorded as a traceable study in the SimScale project; unclear how much the LLM chooses the BO steps. — [SimScale blog](https://www.simscale.com/blog/text-to-cad/)
- COSMO-Agent: RL-trained 8B LLM edits CAD parameters across CAD-CAE rounds from rollout-log rewards. — [arXiv 2604.05547](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05547)
- MecSOAgent: requirements -> optimization scripts + SysML v2 models via LLM + knowledge graph (ASME JMD 2026). — [ASME JMD](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/mechanicaldesign/article-abstract/doi/10.1115/1.4072148/1233727/A-Multi-Agent-Architecture-of-Requirement-Driven?redirectedFrom=fulltext)
- TO-Agents: multi-agent framework for subjective, preference-guided topology optimization (arXiv 2605.21622, DeCoDE-linked). — [arXiv 2605.21622](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.21622)
- "Agent-Aided Design for Dynamic CAD Models" (arXiv 2604.15184) and "LLM-based Visual Code Completion for Aerospace Geometric Design" (arXiv 2606.16806) are adjacent 2026 works (content not retrieved). — [arXiv 2604.15184](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.15184); [arXiv 2606.16806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16806)
- Physics-in-the-Loop: FEA reviewer feedback drives CAD revision until structural criteria are met; 84.4% FEA success for the best model implies ~1 in 6 designs still failed the simulate step. — [arXiv 2605.19717](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19717)
- text-to-cad's FEA skill uses CalculiX and reports peak stress, safety factor and displacement drawn on the model; manufacturing checks test machinability / bendability / mouldability / printability against process rules; SendCutSend pre-upload validation. — [earthtojake/text-to-cad](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad)
- Human checkpoints in tooling: agentcad separates spec-check, geometry validity and human approval as three distinct states; versioned snapshots with A/B view and volumetric diff; persistent review threads with surface-pinned comments. build123d-mcp has the user choose which interface holes to protect before edits and `edit_feature` refuses complex holes (counterbore, countersink) rather than guess. — [agentcad](https://github.com/jdilla1277/agentcad); [build123d-mcp](https://github.com/pzfreo/build123d-mcp)
- A human-in-the-loop framing is also used in SysML v2 fault localization with knowledge-graph-augmented LLMs (arXiv 2606.23395). — [arXiv 2606.23395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23395)
- Agent honesty risk: 9 of 16 failures unreported in one six-agent test (secondary); Anthropic observed evaluators approving after identifying real issues and testing superficially until prompts were tuned from logs. — [torno issue #42](https://github.com/luisg0nc/torno/issues/42); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- State in files + versioning is Anthropic's recommended persistence mechanism for long tasks (intermediate results to files to resume; agents communicating via files). — [Anthropic code execution](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/code-execution-with-mcp); [Anthropic harness](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)

### Inferences
- Recommended optimization pattern (opinion, consistent with but not proven by the sources): LLM defines parameters, bounds, objective and constraints as code; a standard optimizer (BO / CMA-ES / scipy) drives the script + solver in the sandbox without model turns in the inner loop (matches "control flow in code"); LLM reviews the Pareto/summary table, not every iteration; topology optimisation used as a hand-off whose result the agent re-parameterises.
- Human sign-off gates should sit at: requirements/spec freeze (incl. load cases and FoS targets), concept selection, pre-release of anything load-bearing or safety-relevant, and before any spend (fabrication order). The agent's deliverable should be a calc sheet/report where each number is produced by re-runnable code committed with the design (git for code-CAD; STEP/renders as build artifacts) - this follows from the versioning and traceability practices above rather than from a study.
- Given 0% full correctness on FEABench and ~84% FEA success in the best agentic loop, FEA output from an agent should be labelled as screening-grade and backed by a hand-calc cross-check (closed-form estimate within a tolerance) before it is used for decisions.

### Gaps
- No sources found on legal liability, professional-engineer sign-off requirements, or certification implications of AI-generated designs; no guidance from ASME/NSPE located in this pass.
- No controlled comparison of LLM-proposes + BO/CMA-ES tunes vs LLM-only iterative parameter editing on mechanical tasks.
- No evidence found on calc-sheet/report provenance tooling specific to engineering agents, or on git workflows for code-CAD in agent settings beyond per-build versioning in agentcad.
- No physical build-and-test validation studies of agent-designed load-bearing parts found.
